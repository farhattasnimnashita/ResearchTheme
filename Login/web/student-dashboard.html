STUDENT THESIS HANDBOOK

From your first idea to your final thesis

You do not need to know everything before you begin. Start with a clear question, work consistently and improve your research through your supervisor’s feedback.

This is a general guide. Follow your department’s requirements, deadlines and your supervisor’s instructions.

Your getting-started checklist

Profile → Project idea → Team → Supervisor selection → Proposal → Submit for Review

Remember: Team joined means your team membership is confirmed. Supervisor assigned means a teacher has accepted your supervision request.

01Start with your interests and available time

Choose a research area that interests you, such as Machine Learning, Software Engineering or Networks. List your current skills, the time you can commit and the resources you can access. Choose a topic you can realistically investigate, rather than selecting it only because its title sounds impressive.

Complete your identity, skills and research interests in My Profile.

Complete profile →
02Choose a focused, practical research problem

Define the problem, who it affects and which limitation of existing work you want to address. For example: How reliably can historical weather and case data predict dengue trends in a particular area? Check that suitable data is available and that the work fits your project timeline.

Open Choose Project, enter a title, research area and short description, then save your project.

Choose project →
03Read related work and identify a research gap

Read relevant research papers and record each paper’s problem, method, dataset, results and limitations. Compare several studies instead of relying on one paper. Identify a question that existing work has not adequately answered. Keep complete references for every source you use.

Prepare literature review notes and discuss your proposed research gap with your supervisor.

Literature review →
04Build your team and agree on responsibilities

Choose teammates whose skills, availability and working habits complement yours. Agree on responsibilities for data preparation, implementation, experiments and writing. Sending an invitation does not confirm membership: the invited student must accept it.

Use My Team to send invitations and check confirmed members. Check the Team joined status on your dashboard.

Open My Team →
05Choose a suitable supervisor

Review each teacher’s expertise, research interests and Available or Full status. Choose a supervisor whose interests match your topic. Saving a supervisor selection does not send a request. You must submit your proposal, and supervision is confirmed only when the teacher accepts it.

In Supervisor, choose your project and teacher, then click Send Supervision Request.

Choose supervisor →
06Write and submit your research proposal

Problem Statement: explain the problem and why it matters. Aim and Objectives: state what you want to achieve. Methodology: describe your data, tools, experiments and evaluation approach. Expected Outcomes: explain what you hope to produce without presenting anticipated results as established facts. Scope and Limitations: define what the project will and will not cover. Agree on a timeline and required resources with your supervisor.

Use Save Draft to keep unfinished work. When the proposal is complete and a supervisor is selected, click Submit for Review. A draft has not been sent to the teacher.

Write proposal →
07Follow up on the teacher’s decision

Pending means the teacher has not decided yet. Accept confirms supervision and approves the proposal. Ask for Changes means you should revise the proposal using the feedback and submit it again. If the request is declined, read the feedback and discuss a suitable next topic or supervisor. Avoid sending duplicate requests.

Check your request status and feedback in Supervisor. Dashboard placement status refreshes approximately every 15 seconds while the tab is visible.

Track request →
08Plan milestones and regular meetings

Divide the research into manageable milestones: literature review, data preparation, baseline implementation, experiments, analysis and writing. Assign an owner and deadline to each task. Before meetings, prepare a summary of completed work, blockers and questions. Afterwards, record decisions and next actions.

Use Milestones and Meetings to coordinate the research plan with your supervisor.

View milestones →
09Document experiments and respond to feedback

Record data sources, preprocessing steps, model settings, code versions and results so your experiments can be reproduced. Compare results with a suitable baseline and report weaknesses honestly. Do not publish personal data without permission. Discuss required consent and academic approval with your supervisor before collecting or using sensitive data.

Use Files & Feedback to share work and review comments. After making corrections, include a short note explaining what changed.

Files & feedback →
10Prepare your report, defense and handover

Organize the report around the problem, related work, methodology, results, discussion, limitations and references. Credit the writing, images and code you use from others. Make your research question, approach, evidence and contribution clear in your slides. Test your demonstration and follow your supervisor’s and department’s final submission requirements.

Final checklist: proofread the report, verify references, prepare code and data instructions, check slides, keep a demo backup and complete requested corrections.

Review final files →

If you get stuck

Draft: Your proposal has not been submitted. Supervisor missing: Save your selection in Supervisor. Capacity full: Look for another available teacher or contact the teacher. Changes requested: Edit your proposal based on the feedback and submit it again.

After saving work, reload the page to check that it was retained. Keep a separate copy of work created in tools labelled as previews.
